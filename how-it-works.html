How it works

From a sentence on the sidewalk to a work order on a crew's tablet.

SayCity sits between the people who notice problems and the people who fix them. It does the sorting, translating and paperwork in between, in seconds.

The pipeline

Six things happen after someone speaks.

  1. 01

    Speak

    The resident taps once and describes the problem out loud. They can add a photo. They don't need an account, a case number or the name of the right department.“There's a huge hole in the bike lane on Main.”

  2. 02

    Understand

    Speech is transcribed and translated, and the original is kept alongside the translation so nothing is lost. SayCity pulls out the problem type, the hazard and any detail a crew would ask about.lang=es → en · category=PTH · hazard=cyclist

  3. 03

    Locate

    GPS gives the point. Spoken landmarks like “across from the library” or “by the bus shelter” sharpen it, so crews find the problem on the first trip.42.3601° N, 71.0589° W · ±6 m

  4. 04

    Triage

    Each report gets a severity score. A dark crosswalk next to a school outranks a faded parking line. Repeat reports of the same problem merge into one ticket and raise its priority.severity=critical · school_zone · night · 5 merged

  5. 05

    Route

    The ticket goes to the department that owns that asset, through the 311 and work-order tools the city already uses. Nobody has to retype it.→ Street Lighting Division · WO-55120

  6. 06

    Close the loop

    When the work is scheduled and again when it's finished, the resident gets an update in the language they spoke. Neighbors can see the fix on the public map.status=resolved · notified in es

Questions

Common questions

Does SayCity replace 311?

No. It feeds it. SayCity is a faster, multilingual front door that sends clean, structured tickets into the systems a city already runs.

What happens to my voice recording?

SayCity is designed to keep the transcript and drop the audio once a report is processed. Names and phone numbers mentioned in a report are removed before it reaches the public map.

What if a report is an emergency?

If someone describes a life-threatening situation, like a downed power line or a gas smell, SayCity tells them to call emergency services right away and flags the report for the city's emergency contact.

How does SayCity know which department to send a report to?

During setup, each city maps service codes and areas to the teams that own them. SayCity applies that map to every report automatically, and staff can reassign a ticket in one click.
