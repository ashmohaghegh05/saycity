About SayCity

A city works better when everyone in it can be heard.

The problem

The people who see the hazard are often the last ones asked.

Reporting a broken streetlight or a dangerous pothole usually means finding the right phone number, waiting on hold, or filling out a long English-only form. Many people give up. Many never try.

An English-only form shuts out a lot of people. More than 1 in 5 U.S. residents speak a language other than English at home, and 38% of them speak English less than “very well” (U.S. Census Bureau).

So hazards stay in place longer than they should. A dark corner stays dark. A crack becomes a crater. Someone trips, swerves or falls. Deteriorated roads already cost U.S. drivers $169 billion a year, about $723 each (TRIP, 2026). Meanwhile, city teams work from incomplete information and can't tell which problems are most urgent.

SayCity closes that gap. Anyone, of any background and in any language, can speak up in seconds, and their report reaches the right team with everything needed to act on it.

What we stand for

Four principles behind every decision.

Every language

No resident should need to speak English to report a hazard on their own street.

Voice first

Talking is the most natural way to describe a problem. It also works for people who can't easily read or type.

Private by default

We collect what a crew needs to fix the problem and nothing more. Personal details never reach the public map.

Visible progress

Residents see what happened to their report. Cities see what residents are asking for. Both sides build trust.

The team

Started at Babson College.
